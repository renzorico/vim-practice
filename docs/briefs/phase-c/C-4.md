## The idea (proven in the sim with a static Mason)
`dd` does not move you: it moves the world. The player stands on row 3 (the lip); the shaft below belongs to the Mason, and climbing down into it throws you back. Deleting your own row pulls the shaft up to you, row by row, while the Mason lays new rows above himself. A count deletes faster than he builds; `.` repeats it (Dot teaches it).
Checked: after `dd` the cursor stays at (3,c) and the Mason rises one row; `2j3dd..` raises him 9 rows; a count that would reach his scaffold bumps (fixed); `j` into the shaft fires the trap trigger; `dd` in the chamber bumps.
