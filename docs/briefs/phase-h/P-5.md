# P-5: Rewrite the game's text (clear, human, relatable)
Owns: the text strings in `packages/story/content/*.json` (dialogue `text`, `name` only where a rename is listed below, quest `title` and `objective`, cutscene `text` except the fixed lines below); the `hints` strings in `packages/world/content/*/*.json` (stage and script hints only: no map row, tile rule, trigger, entity or solution may change); and tests under `apps/game/src/**` and `packages/story/src/**` only where they assert old wording (update the expected string; say which files).
Runner: claude-sonnet. Reviewer: the design lead (Opus), then Renzo plays it.
Read `docs/MASTERPLAN.md` section 3 (story bible: keep it), `packages/story/src/types.ts`, `packages/story/src/schema.ts` (line limits), then every file in `packages/story/content/`, in world order (margin, lowlands..., last-page). For each stage, read its `packages/world/content/<stage>/<stage>.json` (the map, the `solution`, the triggers) so every instruction you write matches what the player must do.

## Why
Renzo played it: "texts are kind of nonsense and not relatable". He has not judged the story itself: the plot, the cast, the twist and the quest chain stay exactly as they are. What fails is the writing:
- Riddles instead of goals: "You are standing in a paragraph that the contract misfiled." "This ledger is the port's word. Right now it's the clerk's word."
- Vim jargon used as poetry, so neither the story nor the key is clear: "c is d that leaves you typing".
- The "Dot means:" device: Dot says a cryptic word, then a system line explains it. Two boxes for one idea, every stage.
- People talk like signposts, not people: no wants, no worries, no jokes.
- Too many boxes before the player can move.

## The rules (apply to every string)
1. Every box does one job: a goal (what to do, in what you can see), a how (the key, said plainly), a character beat (what this person wants or fears), or a joke from the situation. A box that does none is cut.
2. Say the goal in things on screen: "the crate by the door", "the wall of rubble", "the shelf with the atlases". Never a metaphor the player must decode.
3. Teach with see, key, result: "That rubble is three stones and yours. 4x clears it in one move." One idea per box. Correct to the letter: every key named must be allowed in that stage and must do what you say (check it against the stage's `solution` and commands; a wrong instruction is the worst bug this pass can add).
4. Vim words are allowed once each, plainly: "a paragraph (a block of rows with an empty row after it)". After that, use the in-world word.
5. Intros: at most 3 boxes before the player can move, and the first one says where we are and what's wrong in plain words.
6. Kill "Dot means:". Dot keeps their voice (one or two words, a beat late, MASTERPLAN 3.2). The explanation goes to the person who would know: the Scribe's notes (speaker `scribe`, text starting "Note:"), or the NPC who is talking. Dot's word must still make sense after the explanation, as a callback, not a riddle.
7. Characters (MASTERPLAN 3.2 voices, sharpened). Give each named NPC a want and one verbal habit, keep it in every line they say, and list them in your report:
   - The Elder: slow, ceremonial, scared of being "unsaved" (says it the way we say cursed).
   - Mira: practical, kind, teases Tilde a little.
   - The Scribe (notes): warm, precise, a little guilty; teaches the why ("You could press `l` forty times. Or `$` once.").
   - Dot: one or two words, a beat late; sometimes "attention".
   - Keepers: one distinct voice each, sad rather than evil (each is missing a row).
   - Villagers and clerks: tired, funny, specific (a dockhand worried about her crates, not "the port's word").
8. Humour from the situation (MASTERPLAN 3.6): people in a world that can be deleted row by row; folk who fear being unsaved; a game that knows nobody can quit Vim. Never mock a failed attempt.
9. Quest titles: short and concrete ("Warn the Lighthouse"). Objectives: an imperative the player can act on now, naming the place or thing ("Clear the rubble in the cut. Stand on it and use x.").
10. Death hints and script hints: the exact next thing to try, one sentence plus the keys.
11. Limits from `schema.ts`: at most 2 rows of 48 characters per box (`\n` for the break; check every one). Fewer boxes is good; do not add boxes to explain more.

## Keep exactly
- Every id, speaker type for keepers and Dot, `then` effect list, reaction, trigger, flag, quest structure and step id. You may remove a line from a dialogue's `lines` or merge two into one; never empty a dialogue.
- NPC display names (no renames).
- The twist seeds (MASTERPLAN 3.4 "Seeded from the first minute"): keep every one where it is now, still never stated outright.
- These lines verbatim: "The Scribe is in another buffer." (every castle), the E325 terminal beat (`E325: ATTENTION`, the swap-file line, the `[O]pen Read-Only...` line), `"folio.txt" 4096L written`, the title "UNSAVED", "Thank you for playing.", "To be continued" wherever it appears.
