# P-15: Graphics (places that look like places)
Owns: a new `apps/game/src/scene/terrain/**` (pure modules + tests), `apps/game/src/scene/FieldScene.ts` (only the hooks to draw it), `apps/game/src/scene/sprites/*.ts` terrain tiles (not characters or portraits), `apps/game/src/scene/palette.ts`, the title screen's markup and CSS in `App.tsx`/`play.css`, and a one-time world banner component. No change to `packages/**`.
Runner: codex. Reviewer: claude-sonnet, then a browser check by the design lead.
Read `docs/briefs/phase-b/_common.md`, `scene/README.md`, `scene/palette.ts`, `tiles.ts`, `camera.ts`, `sprites/index.ts`, `docs/MASTERPLAN.md` 3.3 (worlds).
Original art only: no look, palette, tile or character of an existing game.

## Why (the design lead's screenshots of the Quarry)
Every tile is a flat noise texture cut off in a hard square: water meets dirt with no shore, bedrock has no top or face, the road has no edge. Nothing casts a shadow, so people float. A world is told apart only by its hue. The title is text on black.

## 1. Edges (pure, tested)
`terrain/edges.ts`: `edgesFor(rows, cell, legend)` returns which of 4 sides and 4 corners border a different terrain group (water, wall, road, ground, roof...; derived from the legend's kind and sprite). Drawn as pooled 16x16 overlay images above the tile, under entities:
- Water next to land: a darker lip on the land side, a 1 px foam line that moves on the existing 900 ms water clock (still with reduced motion).
- Walls: a lit top face (4 px) where the cell above is not wall, a shaded front (5 px) where the cell below is not wall, so walls read as solid and tall.
- Road and path: irregular worn edges (deterministic by position).
- An edit that changes a tile updates the edges of its 8 neighbours in the same frame (an `x` shifts the rest of the row; a plank over water gets posts). Full recompute per changed row is fine; nothing per frame.

## 2. Depth
- Soft oval shadow under every actor (stays on the ground during a jump and shrinks with height).
- Walls and roofs cast a 3 px shadow on the cell below.

## 3. Life (cheap, deterministic, never a Vim character)
- Ground variation: 4 variants per ground tile, chosen by a position hash; sparse decoration per world (grass tufts and flowers in the Margin, chalk lines in the Lowlands, pebbles in the Quarry, mushrooms and leaf litter in Wordwood, rope and puddles in Copper Port, loose pages in the Library, crystals in the Caves), under 6% of ground cells, drawn only on walkable ground and never taller than 5 px, so it cannot be mistaken for an obstacle.
- One ambient particle layer per world, at most 24 pooled particles: pollen, mist, dust, leaves, gulls' shadows, floating letters, glints. Off with reduced motion and in dark stages.
- Readability wins: obstacles, items, enemies and the guide markers (P-13) must contrast with the ground by at least 3:1 (a test over the palettes).

## 4. World palettes
Each world gets a 12-colour palette with its own light: dawn Margin, grey-blue Lowlands, amber Quarry, deep green Wordwood, copper dusk Port, candle-lit Library, cold violet Caves, white-paper Last Page. Story meaning: rows deleted by the Unwriter use bare paper in every world.

## 5. Title and world banners
- Title: a drawn scene behind the menu, in the engine's tiles: the Margin at dawn, Tilde looking at the rows of `~`, one row vanishing and coming back every 8 s (still with reduced motion). The menu stays readable (4.5:1).
- Entering a world for the first time in a session: a 1.6 s banner (world number, name, a thin line of its tiles), skippable with any key; the key that skips is not sent to the game.

## Performance and tests
60 fps on the largest map: no per-frame allocation; edge, shadow and decoration images pooled. Tests: edges for a water rectangle, a wall corner, an `x` row shift and a plank row; decorations deterministic and only on walkable ground; particle cap; contrast; banner swallows its key; reduced motion. The full-run App test passes.
