Read `docs/briefs/phase-b/_common.md`, `docs/MASTERPLAN.md` 3.2 (cast and voices), `apps/game/src/scene/sprites/sprite.ts`, `index.ts`, `Portrait.tsx`, `player.ts`, `elder.ts`, `mira.ts`, and `story-ui/DialogueBox.tsx`.

## Why
Renzo: "characters are too plain". Today a portrait is the 16x16 field sprite blown up to 64 px (one face, no expression), most villagers share one body shape with a palette swap, and a dialogue box is a grey card with text that appears all at once.

## 1. Portraits (new art, 32x32, same `PixelSprite` format and palette style)
- New folder `scene/sprites/portraits/`: head-and-shoulders portraits at 32x32, drawn as strings like the field sprites (you may extend the palette with a few skin, hair and accent tones; keep the World palettes' feel).
- Three moods per main character: `neutral`, `worried`, `glad` (frames 0, 1, 2 of one `PixelSprite`): eyes and mouth change, plus one prop or gesture where it helps (the Elder's hands clasped when worried).
- Main cast with three moods: the Elder, Mira, Dot (small, round, ink-dark, big eyes), the Scribe (no face: a note card with a quill; moods = ink blot / steady / small flourish), Foreman Ash, Wren, Echo, the Lamplighter, the Mason, Shut, Ope, Tare, Ream, Bitt, Index, Quire, Folio, the Notary, Pilcrow. Everyone else who speaks (Fern, Clerk, Semi, Gull, Kit, Bram, Pell, Cleat): one neutral portrait each, still distinct (hair, hat, tool).
- Each character needs a silhouette you can tell apart at a glance: hat or hair shape, one prop (Ash: pickaxe and dust; Wren: one boot; Mira: bucket; the Lamplighter: lamp; the Mason: trowel; Index: spectacles and cards; the Notary: seal and stiff collar). The Unwriter never gets a face.
- `Portrait.tsx`: render the portrait for the speaker (map display names to portrait ids, case-insensitive, without "The "), at 96 px, crisp (`pixelated`), with the mood frame. Fallback: the field sprite as today. `system` lines: no portrait.

## 2. Mood (pure, tested)
`story-ui/mood.ts`: `moodFor(line: Line, dialogueId?: string): 'neutral' | 'worried' | 'glad'`. Rules: an override table (`Record<dialogueId, mood[]>`, may start empty) wins; otherwise text heuristics: `worried` for words of loss or threat (gone, Unwriter, unsaved, sorry, lost, can't, blank, `...`), `glad` for thanks, done, open, saved, cleared, lit, or an exclamation that is not a warning; else `neutral`. Tests over real content lines (at least 12, from 6 stages).

## 3. Field sprites (16x16, two idle frames, same sprite ids)
Redraw the speaking villagers and keepers so no two share a body: different heights, hats, hair, props, as in section 1, and give the idle frames a real motion (a blink, a breath, a prop moving) rather than a two-pixel shift. Keep every sprite id, size and frame count (the scene and its tests read them). Redraw the player (Tilde) too: a kid with a scarf (the `~` in the name: a wavy scarf that flutters in frame 1), readable at 16 px against grass, stone, paper and darkness.

## 4. Dialogue box
- Speaker name in a colour per character (from the portrait's accent tone).
- Text reveals at 60 characters per second (cosmetic only): Enter advances exactly as today, even mid-reveal (no extra key press anywhere: the session and every test press Enter once per box). Screen readers get the full text at once (the live region holds the full text; the reveal is a visual layer). Reduced motion: no reveal.
- A small bounce of the portrait (2 px, 150 ms) when a new box opens; none with reduced motion.

## Tests
- Every speaker name in `packages/story/content` maps to a portrait (or is `system`/`sign`); a test walks the content.
- Portraits: 32x32, every row 32 chars, 3 frames for the main cast, 1 for the rest, only palette keys used.
- Field sprites: same ids, sizes and frame counts as before (a test compares with a list); no two speaking characters share the same frame 0.
- `moodFor` cases; the reveal does not change `onFinish`/advance behaviour (one Enter per line); reduced motion shows full text.
- Existing tests pass (`story-ui.test.ts`, the full-run App test).
