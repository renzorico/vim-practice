## Why
The game has sound effects but no music: between two keys it is silent, so places feel the same. A quiet, looping, original score per world gives each place a mood and makes thinking time pleasant. It must never tire the player: sparse, soft, slow.

## 1. The scores (data, `score.ts`)
Original compositions only: do not quote or imitate any existing melody. One score per world plus the title/opening and the ending. A score is data: `{ bpm, steps (per bar), bars, voices: [{ wave, level, attackMs, releaseMs, notes: [{ step, midi, length }] }], ambience? }`. 16-bar loops or longer (two 8-bar sections A B, with a varied A': `A A' B A'`), so the loop is at least 60 s at its tempo. At most 4 voices sounding at once: a soft pad or drone (triangle), a plucked line (triangle or sine with a short decay), a sparse bass (sine or triangle), and an optional bell. No square waves louder than half of the others; everything through one low-pass filter (about 2.2 kHz) so effects stay in front.
Moods (write each in its own mode and tempo):
- **title/opening:** D dorian, 66 bpm, slow arpeggio, one unanswered question at the end of the phrase (leave the last bar open).
- **margin:** G mixolydian, 84 bpm, a village: plucked melody with gentle swing, warm bass.
- **lowlands:** C lydian, 72 bpm, wind: long pad, few notes, wide leaps (the key taught is a jump).
- **quarry:** A minor pentatonic, 92 bpm, a steady low knock (a soft filtered noise tick every second beat), short motif repeated and moved.
- **wordwood:** E aeolian, 76 bpm, two plucked voices answering each other (find and repeat: the second repeats the first's last three notes).
- **copper-port:** F dorian, 100 bpm in 6/8, a rocking line.
- **library:** Bb lydian, 60 bpm, bells and silence.
- **echo-caves:** F# phrygian, 56 bpm, a single line and its echo (a real delayed repeat written in the data, quieter), drone.
- **last-page:** the title theme, bare: one voice, half speed, and the phrase is answered at last.
Text stages keep their world's score at 60% level.

## 2. The player (`music.ts`)
- Uses the synth's `AudioContext` and master (one context in the game). A music bus gain (default 0.30 of the master's effects level) feeds the low-pass, then the master.
- A look-ahead scheduler (a 200 ms timer scheduling the next 600 ms on the audio clock). Never per-frame. Nodes disconnect when done. At most 24 live nodes; when the tab is hidden, stop scheduling and fade out; resume at the next bar when visible.
- `setScore(id | null)`: same id is a no-op; a new one cross-fades over 1.5 s (old fades out, new starts at its bar 1).
- `duck(on)`: while a dialogue, the notebook or a card is open, the bus drops to 55% over 300 ms and returns over 800 ms. On death: cut over 200 ms, and restart the score after the retry.
- Muting, a missing `AudioContext`, or a throw anywhere leaves the game silent and working.

## 3. Hook (`useMusic.ts`, one call in `App.tsx`)
Chooses the score from what is on screen: title/opening/stage list → title; in a stage → its world; the ending → last-page. Starts only after the existing unlock gesture.

## 4. Settings
Sound has three states now: `Sound: on` (effects and music), `Sound: effects only`, `Sound: off`. The same button cycles them (title and footer; `aria-label` says the state; `aria-pressed` is removed because it is no longer a toggle). Saved in the existing key with a backward-compatible format (old `on`/`off` values still load). Tests for the old values.

## Tests
- Every score: loop length at least 60 s; notes inside the mode listed (a table of allowed pitch classes per score; at most 2 passing notes outside it, marked in the data); at most 4 simultaneous voices; every note within MIDI 36 to 96; no two scores share an identical 8-note interval sequence in their lead voice.
- Scheduler with a fake clock and fake context: schedules once per note across two loops, no duplicates after a hidden/visible cycle, cross-fade, duck, node cap, disposal.
- `useMusic` choice table. The full-run App test must not need audio.
