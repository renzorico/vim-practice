## Why (the design lead's playthrough of the Quarry Road, where Renzo got stuck)
- How a key works is told once, inside an NPC's chatter ("We don't dig here. We delete."), split over four boxes, and then gone. Instructions do not look like instructions.
- "Stand on the bank, press 5r=": nothing shows which cell is "the bank". `r` lays planks from your cell to the right, so standing two cells away leaves the last water tile, and nothing tells you why.
- After `5r` the game waits for a character, silently. A player who does not know `r` takes a character thinks the key did nothing.
- Bumping into the Scribe's note says "Blocked: someone is here." It should say how to read it.
- `4x` on row 6 shifts the rest of that row 4 cells left, so the river on that row jumps out of line with the rows above and below: the scene stops looking like a river exactly when the player must judge where its bank is.

## 1. Key cards (the how, shown once, properly)
A key is introduced in the first stage (story order) whose objectives, dialogue or hints first name it; a whole world's command list often unlocks in its first stage while some keys are only used stages later, so do not go by `STAGE_COMMANDS` alone. Put the mapping in an app-side table `introducedAt: Record<key, stageId>` with a test that every taught key has one and that each is allowed in that stage. When a stage starts that introduces keys, show one Key card before play (after the intro dialogue, before the first turn): for each new key (at most 3 per card, the rest in a second card), a large keycap, P-12's one-line description, and its before/after example animated: the example row in the game's monospace font, the cursor cell highlighted, the keys typed one by one (400 ms each), then the result row, looping until dismissed (static with reduced motion). Enter or Esc continues. No turn spent. Replay a stage (P-10 Stages or the card's `r`): the card shows again only if the stage is the furthest reached and not yet cleared. `:help` lists the same entries (it already does).
- Tests: the Quarry Road start shows a card with `x` and `r` (not `dd` or `u`, which later Quarry stages introduce); a stage with no new keys shows none; dismiss spends no turn; replay after clear shows none.

## 2. The guide: what to do, where to stand
A pure `guides.ts`: `guideFor(questId, stepId)` returns `{ keys: string[]; standAt?: readonly Position[] } | null`. Write a guide for every main-quest step whose objective asks for a specific key (scan `packages/story/content/*.json`; about 25 of 52 do), and only those: later puzzle steps that do not name a key get none (finding the move is the puzzle).
- HUD (`ObjectiveHud`): under the objective, a "Do:" row: a small stand marker icon (if `standAt`) and the keys as keycaps (`<kbd>`, one per key: `5` `r` `=`). The objective text itself does not change.
- Map: each `standAt` cell gets a dashed outline in the World's accent colour, pulsing at 1 Hz (static with reduced motion), drawn under entities. P-9's arrow points at the nearest `standAt` cell when a guide has one (otherwise P-9's trigger target as today). Hidden in dark stages, on text stages, during cutscenes and when the step is done.
- When the player stands on a `standAt` cell, the outline turns solid and the "Do:" keycaps brighten: "you are in the right place".
- Tests: every guide's keys are allowed in its stage; every `standAt` cell is walkable at the moment that step becomes current (replay the stage's learner route from its test file, or the solution, up to the step); typing the guide's keys from a `standAt` cell completes or advances the step (engine-checked, at least for the Margin, Lowlands and Quarry steps); the Quarry river step's `standAt` is the column of cells directly left of the river.

## 3. showcmd and the pending prompt
Vim shows typed-but-unfinished keys at the bottom right (`showcmd`). Do the same:
- Footer, right of the keycaps: the engine's `pendingKeys` (e.g. `5r`), monospace, accent colour; empty when nothing is pending.
- A short prompt next to it while the engine waits: after `r`: "type the character to write"; after `f`/`t`/`F`/`T`: "type the character to find"; after `d`/`c`/`y` (operator-pending): "now a motion: w, $, j..." (only motions the stage allows); after a count alone: "count: now a command"; after `m`/`` ` ``/`'`: "type a letter". A pure `showcmd.ts` maps pending keys to the prompt; tests per case.

## 4. Range preview for counted `x` and `r`
While `pendingKeys` is `[count] r` (waiting for the character), highlight the cells it will replace: from the cursor rightward, `count` cells (1 without a count). If the line is too short (Vim refuses: nothing happens), highlight them in red and show the prompt "not enough room: count too big". No preview for a bare count (it is ambiguous until the command arrives). Tests in the scene with a fake world: the highlighted cells for `5r` at the Quarry bank are exactly the four water cells and the bank cell.

## 5. Keys look like keys everywhere
A small shared `KeyText` component renders text between backticks as inline keycaps (`<kbd>`; the backticks are never shown and do not count toward the reveal length or the blip cadence). Use it in `DialogueBox`, `ObjectiveHud`, hints, death hints and the clear card: wherever story or hint text is shown. Text without backticks renders exactly as today. An unmatched backtick shows as plain text, because `` ` `` is also a Vim key (marks): write it as a doubled pair, as Markdown does (``` `` ` `` ```). P-14 (a text rewrite, in progress) adds the backticks to the content; until it merges nothing changes visually. If a dialogue box contains keycaps, give it a "Try this" label above the text and the accent-colour left border, so an instruction is recognisable before it is read. Tests: "Press `4x` now." gives "Press ", a kbd "4x", " now."; the doubled form; the label only when a keycap is present; the reveal never shows a backtick or half a keycap.
