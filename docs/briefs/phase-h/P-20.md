Read `docs/briefs/phase-b/_common.md`, `docs/VISUAL-DESIGN.md`, `docs/briefs/phase-h/P-15.md` and `P-18.md` if present, `scene/README.md`, `scene/terrain/{art,edges,surfaces,AtmosphereLayer,atmosphere}.ts`, `scene/sprites/tall.ts`, `scene/sprites/characters.ts`, `scene/animate.ts`.

The fake Phaser in `FieldScene.test.ts` creates mocks lazily; keep it so.

## Why (the design lead's play of merged main)
- The player is one drawing that slides. Villagers never move or turn. Nothing looks alive.
- Columns of trees look like stacked totem poles: each crown is an upside-down trapezoid with a trunk showing between it and the next. The top row of trees shows only trunks.
- Every house is a strip of identical cells: 3 or 4 tiny doors in a row, no chimney, the same red roof.
- The first line of the game is "This morning a whole row of the village is gone. Your house too." and nothing on the screen shows it.
- The surroundings of the Glades and the Reading Room are a hard repeating grid (dark rectangles; identical bookcases) that pulls the eye away from the path.

## 1. Characters
- **Player (Tilde):** original drawings facing down, up, left and right (right is the mirrored left). Two walk frames per direction plus standing. Facing follows the last horizontal or vertical movement (a jump that is more columns than lines faces sideways); a blocked bump also turns the player toward the bump. Start: facing down. While a walk playback is drawn, frames alternate every half cell; standing otherwise. Keep the scarf, the hat and the colours of today's sprite so she is recognisable.
- **People (every entity that is an NPC, not props or enemies):** a second idle frame (breathing: 1 px, 1.2 s cycle, phase per id via the existing `idlePhase`), and they turn left or right to face the player when the player is within 3 cells on the same or the next row (mirroring; no new art).
- Where a playback today relies on frame indexes of the player sprite (dust, death, blink), keep it working.
- Reduced motion: facing still changes; no walk or idle frames.

## 2. Bubbles and labels
- When the objective target (P-9's arrow) is a person, draw a small paper bubble with an ink "!" above the head instead of the chevron. Props, cells and off-screen targets keep the chevron.
- The label next to something interactable says "Enter: read" for notes, signs, books and plaques, "Enter: talk" for people, and "Enter: use" for anything else (today it always says talk). Use the same mapping as `apps/game/src/play2/feedback.ts` (import it, do not copy it). Draw the label on a paper slip with ink text, readable, never clipped by the field's edge or drawn over the player.

## 3. Forest
Trees read as a forest, not poles. Choose a tree's drawing from its neighbours:
- A tree with a tree directly below: crown only, extending down to meet the next one (no trunk, no gap). A tree with no tree below: crown plus trunk.
- A tree with a tree to the left or right: crowns join (no dark outline between joined crowns).
- A lone tree: the round tree of today.
- Three crown variants chosen by a hash of the cell (light clumps at different places, one slightly darker), so rows do not repeat.
- The crown of a tree on the first map row extends above the map into the surroundings.
- Each world that uses `tree` keeps its own palette: crowns must belong to that world's ground (Lowlands: lighter, dry).
- Bake by shape (neighbour mask x variant), not by cell.

## 4. Houses
Treat each rectangle of connected `house-wall` cells as one building: roof with a ridge and eaves over the top rows, one front wall on the last row, one door (the cell nearest the middle), windows in the other front cells with a warm light, a chimney on one side with a slow 3-puff smoke (none with reduced motion), one of three roof colours from a hash of the rectangle's origin, a contact shadow. The walkable cells and the buffer do not change; a building whose cells are edited (none today) falls back to plain cells. Test: the building finder on the Margin finds its three houses (4x2, 3x2, 3x2).

## 5. The Margin: the erased row, and a village you can read
- **The erased row:** in stage `margin` only, buffer line 8, columns 1 to 16 (the village side) is drawn as bare paper with torn, uneven top and bottom edges, a faint pencil outline of a house (roof and door) across columns 6 to 9, and the `~` mark at its left end. It is a drawing over walkable grass: walking on it works as before, actors and shadows draw above it, source view hides it. It is listed in one table (`scene/terrain/erased.ts`: stage id, line, columns), not hard-coded in the scene.
- **Wear and decoration:** a soft worn-dirt patch in front of each door and around the well, joined by short worn paths toward the road (low contrast; cosmetic; only on ground cells). More flowers and tufts at the foot of the forest and the houses, fewer in the open. Deterministic (hash), shared textures.

## 6. Surroundings that stay behind
- Forest surroundings (Margin, Wordwood): a continuous canopy mass from the forest crowns in section 3, darker with distance from the map (3 steps), no repeated dark rectangles.
- Library surroundings: shelves of varied width (2 to 4 cells), some with gaps, a ladder or a lamp, hash-chosen; lower contrast than the playable room.
- Test: for both, no two adjacent 4x4-cell blocks are identical.
