## Why (the design lead's play of merged main, 1024x768)
- The header is 84 px, grows to 98 px, then 124 px as the objective and guide rows appear. The map is resized under it each time, and at this size the top rows of the Margin (a house roof, the Save Stone) are cut off.
- The header, footer and dialogue are dark rounded web-app boxes. The notebook (paper, ink) is the one piece of UI that belongs to this world.
- The footer is a pile: in the Reading Room 14 keycaps on two rows, `:e!`, the blocked message, the Tab hint and the sound button all compete. "Nothing here to talk to or read." stays there long after it stopped being true.
- The dialogue box covers the footer and, when the player stands low on the map, the player.

## 1. A header that never changes height
Two rows, total height fixed (80 px at >= 900 px wide; it may be taller, but still constant, below that):
- Row 1: stage title, then in small muted type the journey line ("World 2 of 6 · Find the Scribe"), then hearts; right-aligned: turn and par. World gauges (`CounterGauge`, `NotaryGauge`, `HoofprintGauge`) sit in this row too.
- Row 2, the quest strip: paper background, ink text, full width: the main objective on the left, the guide ("Stand on [marker] then `5` `r` `=`") on the right. Side objectives, if any, show as "+1" with the full list in a `title`/accessible list (still readable by screen readers). Long objectives wrap to at most 2 lines inside the fixed height with a smaller size; nothing is ever cut: add a test over every objective in `packages/story/content/*.json` that its length fits a documented character budget (pick the budget from the 900 px layout; report any that do not fit, do not edit story text).
- The strip is present (empty) even without an objective, so the field never resizes. A new objective gets one short ink-in animation (static with reduced motion).

## 2. The footer is Vim's status line
One row, fixed height (40 px), monospace, like a real Vim status line:
- Left: a mode block: ` NORMAL `, ` INSERT `, ` VISUAL `, ` V-LINE `, ` V-BLOCK `, ` PENDING `, ` COMMAND ` (from the engine's mode; each with its own colour from the tokens), then the buffer name `.folio.txt.swp` (muted; see `BUFFER_NAME`) and, on text stages, the line and column.
- Middle: the keys learned, newest last, at most 6 keycaps, then "+N" for the rest; clicking "+N" or `:h` opens help. On narrow widths show fewer, never a second row.
- Right: the pending keys and their prompt (P-13 showcmd), then small: `Tab` file view, `:h` keys, and `:e!` start over only after it is taught; the sound button as an icon.
- While typing on the command line (`:`, `/`, `?`) the left half shows the command line as Vim does.
- Keep the accessible names the tests use ("Learned commands", "Pending keys") and the `learnedKeycaps` function's contents.

## 3. Messages near the action
The blocked reason and "Press Enter to read." line become a toast: a small paper slip at the bottom centre of the field, above the status line, `aria-live="polite"`. It appears on the event, fades after 2.5 s, is replaced at once by a newer one, and is removed on the next successful turn. It never sits under a dialogue. Reduced motion: no slide, same timing.

## 4. The dialogue belongs to the book
- Paper panel, ink text (contrast at least 7:1), square corners, a thin ink border and a 3 px darker bottom edge; same family as the notebook.
- Portrait in a framed square on the left at 2x its current size (pixel-perfect: integer scale); the speaker's name on a tab attached to the top edge, in the speaker's accent as the tab background with readable text.
- Narration (no speaker) has no portrait and is centred italic.
- Instruction boxes (those with keycaps, P-13): the left margin becomes a ruled, tinted strip, the label "Try this" is a stamped tag, and the keycaps are drawn as physical keys (raised bottom edge) at 1.15em. It must be recognisable as an instruction at a glance, without reading.
- "Enter to go on" becomes a small blinking down-triangle plus the `Enter` keycap (no blink with reduced motion). Still a real button.
- Placement: the box is inside the field, 16 px above its bottom, never over the status line. If the player's drawn cell is in the lower 45% of the field, the box goes to the top of the field instead. Compute that with the pure `cameraTarget`/`fieldZoom` helpers from the world state and the field's measured size (a pure `dialogueSide.ts` with tests: player at the top of a tall map, at the bottom, and a map smaller than the view). The side is decided when a dialogue opens and does not flip between its lines.
- Text stages, cutscenes, the clear card and death panel keep working. Restyle the clear card and death panel to the same paper; keep their text, layout order and keys.

## 5. The desk (text stages)
`ScriptPanel`: the buffer is a sheet of paper on a dark wooden desk (CSS only: no images, no blur filters): the sheet has a left ruled margin with the line numbers, faint horizontal rules at the line height, a thin shadow. The target column is a second, smaller sheet pinned beside it ("should read"), matched rows ticked and struck in green-ink. The goal sentence is at the top of the sheet like a heading. Keep the fixed gutter, the horizontal scrolling, every aria name, the stamp, and the per-stage colour variants (`script-panel--contract` and others): each must still be readable (contrast test or a listed measurement).

## 6. Do not
No new dependency, no new font, no image files, no emoji. Colours only as tokens in `ui/tokens.css`.

## Tests

Toast: appears, replaced, cleared on the next turn, gone after 2.5 s (fake timers), not shown while a dialogue is open. `dialogueSide` cases. The existing suite passes; change a test only where it asserts the old layout, and list each change in the report.
