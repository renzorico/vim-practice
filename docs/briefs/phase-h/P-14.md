# P-14: A story worth reading, instructions that read as instructions
Owns: text strings only, in `packages/story/content/*.json` (dialogue `text`, quest `title`/`objective`, cutscene `text` except the fixed lines) and the `hints` strings in `packages/world/content/*/*.json`; `docs/MASTERPLAN.md` section 3 (add 3.7, below); tests that pin old wording. No map row, trigger, entity, solution, id, flag or reaction may change.
Runner: claude-sonnet. Reviewer: the design lead (Opus), then Renzo plays it.
Read `docs/MASTERPLAN.md` section 3, `docs/briefs/phase-h/P-5.md` (its rules and its "Keep exactly" list still hold unless changed here), `docs/briefs/phase-h/P-13.md` (key cards and guides), `packages/story/src/schema.ts`, then every story file in world order with its stage map and `solution`.

## Why
Renzo, after P-5: "the messages are a bit odd and not so worth to read, when instructions are given they are not very clear to be instructions. Play with the story." He got stuck on the Quarry river. Three causes:
1. **No spine.** Every box is a local joke. Nothing asks a question the player wants answered, so no box is worth reading.
2. **Instruction and character are mixed in one sentence** ("Stone doesn't argue, child. Stand in front of the rubble and say 4x. Three plus you."). The player cannot tell which words matter.
3. **Verbal tics as personality** (Wren's nine "sorry"s, Ash's "Three. Three."). A tic is funny once.

## 1. The spine: the Scribe's pages (add as MASTERPLAN 3.7)
The Scribe wrote Folio for one reader: their little brother, who asked for the next page every night. He grew up and stopped asking. The Scribe kept writing anyway, a little each night, and stopped saving, because nobody was waiting for it. That is why so much was never saved. Tilde, walking and reading, is the first reader in years.
- The Scribe's notes become pages of this story, one small piece per world, each adding something the last did not: the brother's first request (Lowlands), the quarry he asked for because he liked machines (Quarry), the night he fell asleep before the end (Wordwood), the port he drew himself, badly (Copper Port), the year he did not ask (Library), the Scribe reading it aloud to an empty room (Echo Caves). In each note, the page comes first; the key comes last.
- It never states the twist (MASTERPLAN 3.4): no "file", "swap", "crash" or "Vim" said as an explanation. Every seed stays where it is.
- The Last Page pays it off in the lines that are not fixed: the Scribe realises someone has been reading.
- The Margin's first minute gives Tilde a personal stake: a row of the village they know is gone this morning, and one person is missing from a conversation that still leaves a gap for them. Use only existing characters and dialogues; the missing person is never named in a new id.
- Each world's first box says what Tilde needs here in one sentence; each world's last box answers it and opens the next question.

## 2. Instruction boxes
- A box is either **story** or **instruction**, never both.
- Instruction boxes are the last box of their dialogue, so the last thing read is what to do. One per lesson. Form: `Where. Keys. What you will see.` Example: "Stand on the last dirt before the water.\nPress `5r=`: five planks, across."
- Every key the player must press is in backticks, everywhere (dialogue, objectives, hints, death hints), and nothing else is. The backticks count toward the 48-character row in the schema: keep rows short.
- The place is named by what is on screen and is exact ("the last dirt before the water", not "the bank"). Check each against the map: where must the cursor be, and what does the count cover? Count cells from the map; never guess.
- No "press X and you will see" for keys a later puzzle asks the player to find (P-13: puzzles without guides stay puzzles).
- Delete any explanation the Key card gives (what `x`, `r`, `w` do in general). Dialogue says where and why here.
- Objectives: verb, thing, keys last: "Cross the river: `5r=` from the last dirt." Max 60 characters.

## 3. Voices
- At most 3 boxes per conversation, 2 in an intro. Fewer is better; merge or cut.
- Each named character wants one concrete thing that has nothing to do with the player, and their lines show it changing across their dialogues (first talk, "again", after the stage). List them in your report, one line each.
- A verbal habit may appear once per dialogue at most.
- Plain modern speech, contractions, short words. The test: would you say it to a friend? Humour from losing work nobody saved, which every reader has done.
- Fixed lines from P-5 "Keep exactly" stay verbatim. Dot keeps one or two words, a beat late.
