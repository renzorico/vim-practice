## The idea (proven in the sim, with the clerk's puts emulated)
The counting house. The clerk, who lost the row that said which crates were real, sits at his ledger and copies fakes. Every 2 turns he puts one fake crate `f` right after himself (behaviour `put`, `every` 2). When his ledger holds eight fakes he files it, and the port believes him: the attempt is lost. You must fill three crates on one row first: 8 oil, 8 rope, then 4 oil and 4 rope.
- The samples are single cells: oil at (3,1), rope at (5,1), on the only way down. `"ayl` and `"byl` take one of each.
- Counts on `p` are the lesson: `"a8p` puts eight oil in one turn (the cursor lands on the last one, so `f[` finds the next crate). `.` repeats a counted put (`"a4p .`).
- The goal is one `text` trigger on the crate row's anchor (all three crates at once, so a retry can never leave a crate's story flag stale). The fuse is a `text` trigger on the clerk: eight `f` after him.
- His row is out of reach (he stands between it and the stairs, solid). `u` can take back his puts (entity changes are on the undo stack): known and accepted, it costs you as many turns as it costs him.
Measured: par 12, `j "ayl 2j "byl 2j f[ "a8p f[ "b8p f[ "a4p "b4p` (6 fakes when it clears). With `.` (`"a4p .`): 14. Without registers (the 4-2 commands plus counts): 18, and the fuse fires at turn 16. Without counts, the first crate alone takes 11 turns. The session solver cannot reach depth 12 here: par is a replay test.

## 1. World: the `put` behaviour and the `hit` effect
- `put`: when the entity acts, insert `text` into its row right after its cell (as `p` would with the cursor there). The entity stays where it is; everything right of it on the row moves right (rule 3 remapping); `rows-changed` by 'entity'; on the undo stack. Schema: `text` non-empty, no `\n`.
- `hit`: takes `hearts` (positive int), emits `player-hit` by 'effect'; at 0 hearts the status is 'dead'. Ignored once 'cleared'. A batch that leaves the player dead stores no checkpoint. Mirror it in the story schema's world effects.
Tests: `put` inserts after the entity every `every` turns, shifts the rest of the row (entities and the player on it move with their cells), and `u` takes the last put back; `hit` 1 costs a heart, `hit` 3 kills and the retry returns to the stage start, not to the fuse; `hit` after 'cleared' changes nothing; a schema test for both.

## 2. Engine: a count before a register
Vim accepts the count on either side of the register: `8"ap` is `"a8p`. Today `8"ap` is `unknown-command`. Accept it (and `3"ayl`, `2"bdd`, same rule), with golden tests against Neovim like the other engine cases. Nothing else in the engine changes.
