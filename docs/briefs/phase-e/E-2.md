## The idea (proven in the sim)
The ferry leaves you at the far end of the jetty. Between you and the quay ladder, someone dumped crates (`c`, 5 cells) and nets (`n`, 6 cells) on the boards. Bitt's cart (an anchor entity at (3,4)) runs the boards, and it needs them clear, end to end.
- The boards are fixed and the junk is not walkable. So `x` on a board bumps 'fixed', `x` cannot reach the junk, and `dw`/`de`/`dt.` from a board bump (they would delete the board you stand on).
- `db` from the board just past a pile deletes the pile only (`b` goes to the pile's start and `d` stops short of your cell). The boards slide up to you. `.` repeats it.
- Jumps still fly over junk (only the landing cell is checked), so the junk is not a wall. The goal is the text trigger `jetty.clear`: the 14 characters after the cart are 13 boards and the pier post. Its text ends in `=` on purpose: `13r.` can paint junk into boards, but only a delete makes the walkway the right length.
- Measured: par 6, `b db b . 2<C-u> $ <CR>`. Learner route: `<CR> b db b . 3h 2k $ <CR>` (7 turns). With the World 3 commands (no `d`) the solver finds nothing (10 turns, 40,000 expansions).
Known and accepted (Vim freedom): `d3h` and similar counted deletes also work, and `2<C-u>` reaches the quay without the ladder. Do not block them.

## 5. On screen
When `d` deletes junk: the deleted cells tip into the water (a short splash, about 120 ms), then everything right of them on the row (boards, post, player, entities) slides left into place within the step budget. A rejected `d` bumps, costs no turn, and shows the 'fixed' bump on the cell that would have gone.
