## The idea (proven in the sim)
Ream's warehouse has three bare shelves and a tally: one rack of oil (`[oooo]`) and one of rope (`[rrr]`), the last real stock in the port. The tally stays where it is. You copy it.
- `y` + motion copies and changes nothing. `yw` on the first oil copies `oooo` (the rack's `]` ends the word). `p` puts it just after the cursor: stand on a shelf's `[` and `p` gives `[oooo]`.
- Each rack shares a row with a shelf, so `yw f[ p` stocks it (the jump flies over the rack's `]`, which is not walkable).
- The middle shelf wants oil too but has no rack: the yank is still there, so `p` again. The yank holds one thing, so stock both oil shelves before you copy the rope. The naive order (top, bottom, middle) has to walk back for oil.
- Shelves are text triggers on an anchor entity on the shelf's `[`. Each text ends in `]#`: `yf]` would put a second `]` (and bumps anyway: the cursor would land on the lip), `pp` overfills. Neither matches.
- Cargo is not fixed, so `u` and `x` fix an overfill. The rack's `]` is not walkable, so `4x` on the tally bumps; `3x` works (Vim freedom, it only costs turns).
- Ream stands at (4,13), a cell that only touches the field once the middle or bottom shelf is stocked (the put lengthens the row up to him). Stocking the bottom shelf last leaves you next to him.
Measured par 15: `b 2k l yw f[ p 4h 2j p 4h 2j 3b yw f[ p <CR>`. `y4l` for `yw` is the same length. With the World 1-4 commands (no `y`, `p`) nothing can create cargo, so no route exists.
