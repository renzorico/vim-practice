## The idea (proven in the sim)
The customs shed. Two crates wait for inspection. Tare, the customs officer, clears them only when they hold the right cargo.
- Crate 1 (row 1) sits behind a pile of nets, on the same row as the oil rack. The nets are not walkable and not fixed, as on the Jetty. `yw` the oil, `3w` to the floor past the nets, `db`. Now the unnamed register holds the nets: `p` would put them, and it bumps (the cursor would land on a net). `"0p` puts the last yank. The goal is a text trigger on the customs post (`|` at (1,7)): `....[oooo]#`, so the nets must be gone and the crate filled.
- Crate 2 (row 3) wants oil then rope: `oooorrr]#` after its `[`. The rope rack is far away, on row 5 next to the start. `"byw` the rope first, carry it through crate 1, then `"0p` (or `"ap`) and `"bp` at crate 2.
- A named yank does not touch `"0` (Vim): `"byw` then `yw` leaves rope in `"b` and oil in `"0`. Both `"a`/`"b` and `"0` routes are 16 turns.
- Tare stands at (4,18), a cell that joins the field only when crate 2 holds seven cargo (the put lengthens row 3 up to him), as Ream did in 4-2.
Measured by hand, par 16: `j l "byw h 4k l yw 3w db f[ "0p 8h 2j f[ "0p "bp <CR>`. Without `"` (the 4-2 commands) the best route found is 20 (`3k 4w db 2b yw f[ p 8h 2j f[ p 14h 2j l yw h 2k f[ 4l p <CR>`: it yanks oil after the delete and walks back for the rope). The session solver cannot reach depth 16 (4-2 measured it running out of memory at 15): par is a replay test, not a proof.
