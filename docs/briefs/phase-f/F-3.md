## The idea (proven in the sim)
The reading room floor is one long row. Chains lie across it, each held by a pair of shackles: real brackets in the buffer, `(cccc)`. Chains and shackles are walkable and not fixed; the floor and the posts are fixed. Folio, a reader, sits chained at the far end.
- `w` from the start lands inside the first chain (the floor dots and the `(` are one punctuation word, so `w` skips to the chain links). `da(` deletes the chain and both shackles from anywhere inside them. The floor closes up.
- The second is a chain inside a chain, `((cc)ccc)`. From the inner links, `d2a(` takes both pairs in one go.
- The last is Folio's: `di(` takes the chain and leaves the shackle, open: `()`. Folio's cell slides up next to you; `<CR>`.
- The goal is one text trigger on the door post at the row's start: `.........()..|` (the room after all three, with the open shackle). `da(` on Folio's chain would leave no shackle: no match.
Measured: par 6, `w da( w d2a( w di( <CR>`. With the Stacks commands (no `(` objects) the best route is 9: `w % d% w % h d% w dt) <CR>` (`%` to a shackle, then `d%`; `dt)` for the last). The session solver cannot reach depth 6 here: par is a replay test.
