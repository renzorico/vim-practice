## The idea (proven in the sim)
The Library's stacks. Three shelf rows hang under a gallery, and the shelves are written as text: books (`b`) are words, the gaps between them are real spaces, and words of dust (`d`) got shelved between the books. Books are fixed; dust and gaps are not. Everything on a shelf is walkable.
- From the gallery, `j` drops you into the middle of the first dust word (start (0,12), the dust spans columns 10-14). `daw` deletes the word wherever you stand in it, plus the space after it: the shelf closes up and the cursor lands on the next book.
- That book's column is inside the next shelf's dust: `j` drops you into the middle of it again, and `.` repeats `daw`. The rows are built so the shift of each delete lines up the next drop.
- `diw` takes the word but leaves its space: two gaps where the shelf wants one. Each shelf is a text trigger on a post entity at the row's start whose text ends at the far post `|`, so the spacing counts.
- Quire, the librarian, waits under the stack at (4,7), next to where the last delete leaves you.
Measured: par 6, `j daw j . j . <CR>`. With the World 4 commands (no text objects) the best route is 9: `2h j dw j b . j b . <CR>` (walk to the dust's start first, then `b` back to it on each shelf). The session solver cannot reach depth 6 here (no result at 8 turns / 6,000 expansions even on a 10-command vocabulary): par is a replay test.
