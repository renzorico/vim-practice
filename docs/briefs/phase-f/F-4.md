## The idea (proven in the sim, with the multi-row check emulated)
The archive. Shelves of paragraphs: blocks of rows with blank rows (aisles, the legend's `emptyRow`) between them, under a rail at the top and above a rail at the bottom. You start in the middle of a misfiled block (`x`), between the atlases (`a`) and the bibles (`b`). It belongs after the bibles. And the contract wants a copy of the atlases by the door, above the bottom rail.
- `dap` deletes the paragraph you stand in, wherever in it, and its blank row after. `}` finds the blank row after the bibles; `p` puts the block below it.
- `gg 2j` reaches the atlases; `yap` copies them with their blank row; `G` goes to the bottom rail and `P` puts the copy above it.
- `dip` and `yip` leave the blank row out: two aisles run together, or two blocks touch. The goal is one `text` trigger on the catalogue anchor at the top rail's first cell, whose text runs across every row below it (`\n` between rows), so the order of the blocks and the aisles both count.
- Block rows are all cargo (not fixed): a row with a fixed cell cannot be deleted (World 4). The rails are fixed.
Measured: par 8, `dap } p gg 2j yap G P`. With the Reading Room commands plus `P` and `yy` (no paragraph objects) the best route is 10: `{ j 4dd } p gg 2j 3yy G P` (count the rows). The session solver cannot reach depth 8: par is a replay test.
